Chapter Seven

The Martyrdom

“I have fought the good fight, I have finished the race, I have kept the faith.” — 2 Timothy 4:7

❖ ❖ ❖

I The Final Imprisonment

The New Testament does not narrate Paul’s death. The book of Acts ends with him alive in Rome under house arrest, still preaching freely (Acts 28:30–31). But his last surviving letter, 2 Timothy, reads unmistakably like words written near the end — from a cold cell, abandoned by many, sensing that his execution is near.

“For I am already being poured out as a drink offering, and the time of my departure has come.” 2 Timothy 4:6

II Words from the Edge

In that final letter he asks Timothy to come quickly and bring his cloak and his books (2 Timothy 4:9–13) — small, human details that make the moment vivid. He notes that at his first defense “no one came to stand by me”, yet the Lord stood with him (2 Timothy 4:16–17). His confidence is not in rescue from death but in what lies beyond it:

“Henceforth there is laid up for me the crown of righteousness, which the Lord, the righteous judge, will award to me on that day.” 2 Timothy 4:8

III How He Died

For the manner of his death we rely on early church tradition rather than Scripture. The consistent witness of the early centuries is that Paul was executed in Rome under the emperor Nero, likely around AD 64–67, during or after the persecution that followed the great fire of Rome.

Because he was a Roman citizen, tradition holds that he was spared crucifixion and instead beheaded by the sword — a citizen’s execution — on the road outside the city. The site is traditionally associated with the church now called San Paolo alle Tre Fontane, and he is said to be buried where the Basilica of St Paul Outside the Walls stands today.

Scripture vs. tradition. The Bible records Paul’s expectation of death (2 Timothy 4) but not the event itself. The details of beheading under Nero come from early Christian writers such as Clement of Rome, Eusebius, and Tertullian. See the Resources page for where to read these sources.

IV The Legacy

Paul died as he had lived — certain that to depart was “to be with Christ, for that is far better” (Philippians 1:23). The churches he planted survived; the letters he wrote became Scripture; and the gospel he carried from Jerusalem toward Rome eventually reached the ends of the earth he never lived to see. Few lives have echoed so far.
