Chapter Four

The Teaching

“For I decided to know nothing among you except Jesus Christ and him crucified.” — 1 Corinthians 2:2

❖ ❖ ❖

Paul was not a systematic theologian writing a textbook; he was a pastor answering real questions in real churches. Yet across his letters a coherent vision emerges — one that has shaped Christian thought for two thousand years. The themes below are the load-bearing pillars of his teaching.

Video: Paul’s theology of new life in Christ, drawn from Romans (BibleProject).

I Justification by Faith

At the center of Paul’s gospel is the conviction that no one is made right with God by keeping the law, but by faith in Christ. Salvation is a gift, not a wage.

“For by grace you have been saved through faith. And this is not your own doing; it is the gift of God, not a result of works, so that no one may boast.” Ephesians 2:8–9 · see Romans 3:28; 5:1; Galatians 2:16

II Grace and the Cross

For Paul, the death and resurrection of Jesus is the hinge of all history. The cross reconciles sinners to God and is the very thing he refuses to be ashamed of (Romans 1:16; 5:8). He sums up the gospel he received and passed on in 1 Corinthians 15:3–4: Christ died for our sins, was buried, and rose again.

III Life in the Spirit

The Christian life is not mere rule-keeping but transformation by the Holy Spirit, who produces character: “love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, self-control” — the fruit of the Spirit (Galatians 5:22–23). Believers are no longer slaves to sin but free to walk a new way (Romans 8).

IV The Body of Christ

Paul’s favorite picture of the church is a single body with many members, each gifted differently but indispensable to the whole (1 Corinthians 12; Romans 12:4–5). Unity across deep divisions — Jew and Gentile, slave and free — is a defining mark of his vision (Galatians 3:28).

V Love Above All

His most famous passage sets love above every gift and achievement — the so-called “love chapter” read at weddings the world over:

“Love is patient and kind… So now faith, hope, and love abide, these three; but the greatest of these is love.” 1 Corinthians 13:4, 13

VI Resurrection and Hope

Paul anchors Christian hope in the resurrection of Jesus and the promised resurrection of believers: “if Christ has not been raised, your faith is futile” (1 Corinthians 15:17). Death is the last enemy, already defeated (1 Corinthians 15:54–57), and nothing “will be able to separate us from the love of God” (Romans 8:38–39).

Read the book of Romans. If one letter gathers Paul’s teaching into a single sustained argument, it is Romans — from human sinfulness, through justification and life in the Spirit, to the practical shape of a transformed life. See the Letters page for the full list.
