Chapter Three

The Ministry

“I have become all things to all people, that by all means I might save some.” — 1 Corinthians 9:22

❖ ❖ ❖

I Sent Out

Paul’s public ministry unfolded as a series of missionary journeys radiating out from Antioch in Syria. The church there, prompted by the Holy Spirit, set apart Paul and Barnabas for the work (Acts 13:1–3). Over roughly fifteen years he would travel thousands of miles by land and sea, planting churches in city after city and writing back to strengthen them.

Video: overview of the book of Acts, which narrates Paul’s journeys (BibleProject).

II The First Journey

c. AD 46–48 · with Barnabas

Setting out from Antioch through Cyprus and into the cities of Asia Minor — Pisidian Antioch, Iconium, Lystra, and Derbe — Paul preached first in the synagogues and then to the Gentiles (Acts 13–14). At Lystra he was stoned and left for dead, yet rose and continued (Acts 14:19–20). This journey forced the early church to settle a vital question, which it did at the Council of Jerusalem: Gentiles need not become Jews to follow Christ (Acts 15).

III The Second Journey

c. AD 49–52 · into Europe

After parting from Barnabas, Paul took Silas, and later Timothy, westward. A night vision of a man pleading “Come over to Macedonia and help us” drew the gospel into Europe for the first time (Acts 16:9–10). Highlights include the conversion of Lydia and the Philippian jailer at Philippi (Acts 16), the sermon on the Areopagus in Athens (Acts 17:22–31), and eighteen months in Corinth (Acts 18:11).

IV The Third Journey

c. AD 53–57 · Ephesus

Paul spent some three years in Ephesus, where “all the residents of Asia heard the word of the Lord” and a riot eventually broke out over the threat his preaching posed to the silversmiths of Artemis (Acts 19). From this period flow several of his major letters. He then journeyed back through Macedonia and Greece, determined to return to Jerusalem despite warnings of danger (Acts 20–21).

V The Road to Rome

c. AD 57–62

Arrested in Jerusalem, Paul used his Roman citizenship to appeal to Caesar, setting in motion a voyage to Rome that included a dramatic shipwreck off Malta (Acts 25:11; 27–28). Acts closes with Paul under house arrest in Rome, still “proclaiming the kingdom of God… with all boldness and without hindrance” (Acts 28:30–31).

A traveling pastor. Paul rarely preached and moved on without looking back. He revisited churches, sent coworkers like Timothy and Titus, and wrote letters to nurture young congregations — the ministry continued by ink when he could not be present in person.
