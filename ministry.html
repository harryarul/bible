An Illustrated Study

The Apostle
Paul

“For to me to live is Christ, and to die is gain.”
— Philippians 1:21

❖ ❖ ❖

Saul of Tarsus — the man who would become Paul — began as the fiercest enemy of the early church and ended as its most far-reaching missionary. No figure outside of Jesus himself shaped the New Testament more: roughly half its books are letters bearing his name, and the second half of the book of Acts is essentially the story of his travels. These pages trace his whole arc: the zealous Pharisee, the blinding encounter on the Damascus road, the journeys, the theology, the imprisonments, and the martyr’s death in Rome.

Use the chapters below to move through his life, or jump to his teaching, his thirteen letters, or a list of outside resources for deeper study. Each page includes paintings, maps, and video.

III At a Glance

  • c. AD 5 Born in Tarsus of Cilicia, a Roman citizen by birth (Acts 22:3, 28)
  • c. AD 33–36 Persecutes the church; conversion on the road to Damascus (Acts 9)
  • c. AD 46–48 First missionary journey with Barnabas (Acts 13–14)
  • c. AD 49–52 Second journey; into Greece — Philippi, Corinth, Athens (Acts 15–18)
  • c. AD 53–57 Third journey; long stay in Ephesus (Acts 18–21)
  • c. AD 57–62 Arrest in Jerusalem, imprisonment, voyage to Rome (Acts 21–28)
  • c. AD 64–67 Martyrdom in Rome under Nero (2 Timothy 4)
