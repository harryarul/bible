The Apocalypse of Jesus Christ

The Visions
on Patmos

Every sight John was shown, unrolled in the order he saw it — from the lampstands to the city of gold.

I, John, your brother and partner in the tribulation, was on the island called Patmos for the word of God and the testimony of Jesus. On the Lord's Day I was in the Spirit, and I heard behind me a loud voice like a trumpet. Revelation 1:9–10

I The UnveilingChapter 1

One like a Son of Man

Before any prophecy of the future, John is given a vision of the risen Christ himself — the living centre from whom every later vision flows.

  • 1
    A voice like a trumpet

    In the Spirit on the Lord's Day, John hears a great voice behind him commanding him to write to seven churches in Asia.

    Rev 1:9–11
  • 2
    Seven golden lampstands

    Turning, John sees seven lampstands and, among them, the glorified Lord — hair white as snow, eyes like flame, feet like burnished bronze, a sharp two-edged sword from his mouth, his face like the sun in full strength.

    Rev 1:12–16
  • 3
    "I hold the keys of Death and Hades"

    John falls as though dead; Christ lays his hand on him: "Do not be afraid. I am the First and the Last, the Living One. I was dead, and behold, I am alive forevermore."

    Rev 1:17–18
  • 4
    The commission & the mystery

    Write what you have seen, what is, and what is to come. The seven stars are the angels of the churches; the seven lampstands are the churches themselves.

    Rev 1:19–20
II Letters to the Seven ChurchesChapters 2–3

Words to the lampstands

Christ dictates a letter to each church — a commendation, a rebuke, a call, and a promise to the one who overcomes.

  • 1
    Ephesus — the loveless church

    Hardworking and discerning, yet they have forsaken their first love. Return and do the first works.

    Rev 2:1–7
  • 2
    Smyrna — the persecuted church

    Poor yet rich; about to suffer. "Be faithful unto death, and I will give you the crown of life."

    Rev 2:8–11
  • 3
    Pergamum — the compromising church

    Faithful where Satan's throne is, yet tolerating false teaching. Hidden manna and a white stone await the overcomer.

    Rev 2:12–17
  • 4
    Thyatira — the corrupted church

    Growing in love and service, but tolerating "Jezebel." Hold fast; the overcomer is given authority over the nations.

    Rev 2:18–29
  • 5
    Sardis — the dead church

    A name for being alive, but dead. Wake up. The overcomer is clothed in white and kept in the book of life.

    Rev 3:1–6
  • 6
    Philadelphia — the faithful church

    Little strength, yet they kept his word. He sets before them an open door no one can shut.

    Rev 3:7–13
  • 7
    Laodicea — the lukewarm church

    Neither hot nor cold; rich in their own eyes, but wretched and blind. "Behold, I stand at the door and knock."

    Rev 3:14–22
III The Throne Room of HeavenChapters 4–5

The Lamb who is worthy

A door opens in heaven. Everything that follows is set in motion here — at the throne, and by the one alone found worthy to open the scroll.

  • 1
    The throne and the rainbow

    One seated on the throne, like jasper and carnelian, encircled by a rainbow like an emerald — surrounded by twenty-four elders and seven burning lamps, the seven spirits of God.

    Rev 4:2–6
  • 2
    The four living creatures

    Lion, ox, man, eagle — covered with eyes, never resting: "Holy, holy, holy is the Lord God Almighty, who was and is and is to come."

    Rev 4:6–11
  • 3
    "Who is worthy to open the scroll?"

    A scroll sealed with seven seals; no one in heaven or earth can open it. John weeps much.

    Rev 5:1–4
  • 4
    The Lion who is the Lamb

    "Weep no more — the Lion of Judah has conquered." John turns and sees a Lamb, looking as if it had been slain. He takes the scroll, and all heaven sings a new song.

    Rev 5:5–14

Worthy is the Lamb who was slain, to receive power and wealth and wisdom and might and honour and glory and blessing — to him be praise forever and ever.

Revelation 5:12–13
IV The Seven SealsChapters 6 – 8:1

The Lamb opens the scroll

As each seal is broken, history is set loose — conquest, war, famine, death, the cry of the martyrs, and the shaking of the heavens.

  • 1
    Seal I — the White Horse

    A rider with a bow and a crown, going out conquering and to conquer.

    Rev 6:1–2
  • 2
    Seal II — the Red Horse

    Power to take peace from the earth, so that people kill one another; a great sword is given.

    Rev 6:3–4
  • 3
    Seal III — the Black Horse

    A pair of scales — famine and scarcity: a day's wage for a quart of wheat.

    Rev 6:5–6
  • 4
    Seal IV — the Pale Horse

    Its rider's name is Death, and Hades follows — given power over a fourth of the earth.

    Rev 6:7–8
  • 5
    Seal V — the souls under the altar

    The martyrs cry out, "How long, O Lord?" They are given white robes and told to wait a little longer.

    Rev 6:9–11
  • 6
    Seal VI — the great earthquake

    The sun turns black, the moon to blood, stars fall, the sky rolls back. Kings and slaves alike cry to the mountains, "Hide us from the wrath of the Lamb."

    Rev 6:12–17
Interlude · Chapter 7 Before the seventh seal, a pause: 144,000 are sealed from the tribes of Israel, and John sees a great multitude no one could number, from every nation, in white robes before the throne — those who came out of the great tribulation.
Rev 7:1–17
  • 7
    Seal VII — silence in heaven

    When the last seal is opened, there is silence in heaven for about half an hour — the hush before the trumpets.

    Rev 8:1
V The Seven TrumpetsChapters 8 – 11

Seven angels sound

The prayers of the saints rise with incense; fire is hurled to the earth; and seven trumpets announce a deeper round of judgment.

  • 1
    Trumpet I — hail and fire

    Hail and fire mixed with blood fall; a third of the earth and trees and all green grass are burned.

    Rev 8:7
  • 2
    Trumpet II — the burning mountain

    A great blazing mountain is thrown into the sea; a third of the sea becomes blood.

    Rev 8:8–9
  • 3
    Trumpet III — Wormwood

    A great star called Wormwood falls; a third of the rivers and springs turn bitter.

    Rev 8:10–11
  • 4
    Trumpet IV — the darkened sky

    A third of the sun, moon, and stars are struck dark. An eagle cries: "Woe, woe, woe to those who dwell on the earth."

    Rev 8:12–13
  • 5
    Trumpet V — locusts from the abyss (1st Woe)

    The abyss is opened; locusts like war-horses torment those without God's seal. Their king is Abaddon, Apollyon — the Destroyer.

    Rev 9:1–12
  • 6
    Trumpet VI — the army at the Euphrates (2nd Woe)

    Four bound angels are released; an army of two hundred million kills a third of mankind — yet the rest still refuse to repent.

    Rev 9:13–21
Interlude · Chapters 10 – 11 A mighty angel gives John a little scroll to eat — sweet as honey in the mouth, bitter in the stomach. He measures the temple, and two witnesses prophesy, are killed, lie in the street, and after three and a half days are raised and called up to heaven.
Rev 10Rev 11:1–14
  • 7
    Trumpet VII — the kingdom proclaimed

    Loud voices in heaven: "The kingdom of the world has become the kingdom of our Lord and of his Christ, and he shall reign forever and ever." The temple in heaven opens.

    Rev 11:15–19
VI The Dragon & the BeastsChapters 12–14

The war behind the war

The curtain is pulled back on the conflict itself — a woman, a dragon, and two beasts — and the players in the cosmic struggle are unmasked.

  • 1
    The woman, the child & the dragon

    A woman clothed with the sun gives birth to a son who will rule the nations; a great red dragon waits to devour the child, but the child is caught up to God.

    Rev 12:1–6
  • 2
    War in heaven

    Michael and his angels fight the dragon. The ancient serpent — the devil, Satan — is hurled down to the earth. "They overcame him by the blood of the Lamb and the word of their testimony."

    Rev 12:7–17
  • 3
    The beast from the sea

    Seven heads, ten horns, mouth of blasphemy. The dragon gives it authority for forty-two months, and the whole earth marvels and worships it.

    Rev 13:1–10
  • 4
    The beast from the earth — the mark

    A second beast, like a lamb but speaking like a dragon, forces all to receive a mark to buy or sell. Here is wisdom: its number is 666.

    Rev 13:11–18
  • 5
    The Lamb and the 144,000

    On Mount Zion, the Lamb stands with the redeemed, who sing a new song no one else can learn — the firstfruits, blameless before God.

    Rev 14:1–5
  • 6
    Three angels & the harvest

    Angels proclaim the eternal gospel, announce "Fallen is Babylon", and warn against the mark. Then the earth is reaped, and the winepress of God's wrath is trodden.

    Rev 14:6–20
VII The Seven Bowls of WrathChapters 15–16

The last plagues

By the sea of glass mixed with fire, the redeemed sing the song of Moses and the Lamb — then seven angels pour out the final, unmingled wrath of God.

  • 1
    Bowl I — festering sores

    Poured on the land; ugly, painful sores break out on those who bear the mark of the beast.

    Rev 16:2
  • 2
    Bowl II — the sea to blood

    The sea becomes like the blood of a corpse; every living thing in it dies.

    Rev 16:3
  • 3
    Bowl III — the rivers to blood

    Rivers and springs turn to blood. The angel declares God just: they shed the blood of saints.

    Rev 16:4–7
  • 4
    Bowl IV — scorching sun

    The sun is allowed to scorch people with fire — yet they curse God and refuse to repent.

    Rev 16:8–9
  • 5
    Bowl V — darkness

    Poured on the throne of the beast; its kingdom is plunged into darkness, and people gnaw their tongues in agony.

    Rev 16:10–11
  • 6
    Bowl VI — the Euphrates & Armageddon

    The great river dries to prepare the way for kings from the east; unclean spirits gather them for battle at Armageddon.

    Rev 16:12–16
  • 7
    Bowl VII — "It is done"

    A loud voice from the throne. The greatest earthquake in history; cities fall, islands flee, and great Babylon is remembered to drink the cup of God's fury.

    Rev 16:17–21
VIII The Fall of BabylonChapters 17–18

The great city falls

The seventh bowl named Babylon; now her judgment is shown in full — the gilded harlot of empire, and the dirge of a world that loved her.

  • 1
    The woman on the scarlet beast

    A prostitute arrayed in purple and scarlet, holding a golden cup of abominations, drunk with the blood of the saints. On her forehead: "Mystery, Babylon the Great."

    Rev 17:1–6
  • 2
    The mystery explained

    The seven heads and ten kings make war on the Lamb — "and the Lamb will conquer them, for he is Lord of lords and King of kings."

    Rev 17:7–18
  • 3
    "Fallen, fallen is Babylon"

    An angel of great authority lights the earth with his glory. A voice from heaven calls, "Come out of her, my people, that you take no part in her sins."

    Rev 18:1–8
  • 4
    The lament of the world

    Kings, merchants, and sea-captains weep for their lost trade. A mighty angel hurls a millstone into the sea: so will Babylon be thrown down, never to be found again.

    Rev 18:9–24
IX The Triumph of the LambChapters 19–20

Faithful and True

Heaven erupts in Hallelujahs; the King rides out; the beast, the false prophet, and at last the dragon himself meet the lake of fire, and the dead are judged.

  • 1
    Hallelujah & the wedding of the Lamb

    A great multitude in heaven roars "Hallelujah!" The bride has made herself ready; blessed are those invited to the marriage supper of the Lamb.

    Rev 19:1–10
  • 2
    The rider on the white horse

    Heaven opens. He is called Faithful and True; his eyes are flame, his robe dipped in blood, his name the Word of God. On his thigh: King of kings and Lord of lords.

    Rev 19:11–16
  • 3
    The beast & false prophet judged

    The beast and the false prophet are captured and thrown alive into the lake of fire burning with sulphur.

    Rev 19:17–21
  • 4
    Satan bound — the thousand years

    An angel seizes the dragon, binds him, and casts him into the abyss for a thousand years. The faithful reign with Christ in the first resurrection.

    Rev 20:1–6
  • 5
    The last rebellion & the dragon's end

    Released, Satan gathers Gog and Magog for battle; fire comes down from heaven. The devil is thrown into the lake of fire forever.

    Rev 20:7–10
  • 6
    The great white throne

    Earth and sky flee away. The dead are judged by what is written in the books — and by the book of life. Death and Hades themselves are thrown into the lake of fire.

    Rev 20:11–15
X A New Heaven & a New EarthChapter 21

The city of gold

Every vision of judgment was clearing the ground for this — the home of God with his people, where the old order of tears is gone forever.

  • 1
    The holy city descends

    A new heaven and a new earth; the old has passed. The New Jerusalem comes down from God, prepared as a bride adorned for her husband.

    Rev 21:1–2
  • 2
    "Behold, I make all things new"

    God dwells with his people. He wipes away every tear; death shall be no more, nor mourning, nor crying, nor pain — the former things have passed away.

    Rev 21:3–8
  • 3
    The city of jasper and gold

    Twelve gates of pearl named for the twelve tribes; twelve foundations named for the twelve apostles, each a different jewel. The city is pure gold, clear as glass — a perfect cube.

    Rev 21:9–21
  • 4
    No temple, no sun, no night

    There is no temple — the Lord God and the Lamb are its temple. There is no sun — the glory of God is its light, and the Lamb is its lamp. Its gates are never shut, for there is no night there.

    Rev 21:22–27

He will wipe away every tear from their eyes, and death shall be no more, neither shall there be mourning, nor crying, nor pain anymore, for the former things have passed away.

Revelation 21:4
The vision continues

And the Spirit and the Bride say, "Come."

The scroll does not quite close here. Chapter 22 unrolls the last sight — the river of the water of life, the tree of life with healing leaves, and the final promise: "Surely I am coming soon." A natural place to take this study next.
