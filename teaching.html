Chapter Two

The Damascus
Road

“Saul, Saul, why are you persecuting me?” — Acts 9:4

❖ ❖ ❖

I The Encounter

On the road to Damascus, armed with letters authorizing him to arrest believers, Saul was stopped by the risen Christ. Luke considered the moment so pivotal that he records it three times in Acts (Acts 9, 22, 26).

A light from heaven flashed around him; he fell to the ground and heard a voice. When he asked who was speaking, the answer reframed everything he believed: “I am Jesus, whom you are persecuting” (Acts 9:5). To strike the church was, he now learned, to strike its Lord.

“Suddenly a light from heaven shone around him. And falling to the ground he heard a voice.” Acts 9:3–4

Video: an introduction to Paul and his world (BibleProject). For a dramatized telling of Acts 9, search “Paul’s conversion” on YouTube.

II Blindness and Ananias

Saul rose blind and was led by the hand into Damascus, where he neither ate nor drank for three days (Acts 9:8–9). The Lord then sent a disciple named Ananias — understandably afraid of the famous persecutor — to lay hands on him. Through Ananias, Saul received his sight and the Holy Spirit, and was baptized (Acts 9:10–19).

It was here that his commission was announced. He was to be a “chosen instrument” to carry Christ’s name “before the Gentiles and kings and the children of Israel” (Acts 9:15).

III From Saul to Apostle

The change was immediate and public: “immediately he proclaimed Jesus in the synagogues, saying, ‘He is the Son of God’” (Acts 9:20). Those who heard were astonished — was this not the man who came to destroy them?

Paul understood his calling as a direct revelation, not something handed down secondhand. He spent time in Arabia and Damascus before going up to Jerusalem, insisting the gospel he preached came “through a revelation of Jesus Christ” (Galatians 1:11–18).

One man, two names. Scripture shifts from “Saul” to “Paul” around Acts 13:9, as his mission turns toward the Greek-speaking Gentile world. “Paul” was his Roman name; the change marks the man stepping fully into his life’s work.
